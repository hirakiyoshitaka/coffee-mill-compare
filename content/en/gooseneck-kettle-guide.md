Pour-over needs water poured thin, in the exact spot you aim at. A gooseneck kettle does that job: its spout is thin and long, so you control how much water comes out and where it lands. The water spreads evenly over the grounds, and the taste varies less from cup to cup.

This is a review summary. We have not used any kettle in this guide: each section below follows the maker's pages and published reviews, and links go to Amazon.com search results, so compare sellers and check the price and stock on the day you buy.

## Gooseneck vs a regular kettle

A regular kettle pours fast and wide. A gooseneck spout narrows the stream, so a small tilt changes the flow a little instead of a lot. That is the whole point of the shape: pour slow at the start to bloom the grounds, then keep the stream steady.

## Six things to check

1. **Spout shape**: A thin gooseneck is the baseline. The thinner the tip, the smaller the pours you can make.
2. **Temperature control**: An electric kettle can set the temperature in small steps and hold it. Light roasts take hotter water, dark roasts cooler water, so match the temperature to the beans.
3. **Capacity**: For 1–2 cups, 0.6–0.8 L is the guide; for a family, around 1 L. A full kettle is heavy to pour, so leave some headroom above what you brew.
4. **Heat source**: Electric is easy and controls the temperature. A stovetop kettle needs a check: IH compatible, or gas only.
5. **Weight and handle**: Check whether your wrist tires when the kettle is full. Grip comfort is in the reviews.
6. **Keep-warm and dry-boil protection**: Check the safety functions before you buy.
