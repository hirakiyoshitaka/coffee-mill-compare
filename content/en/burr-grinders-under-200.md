A burr grinder crushes beans between two burrs instead of chopping them, so the grounds come out more even — and even grounds brew more evenly. Under $200, the choice is mostly between a hand grinder and an electric one, and between filter brewing and espresso.

**Quick pick:** for pour-over by hand, the [Timemore Chestnut S3](/en/articles/timemore-chestnut-s3/) — the one I usually use. For drip and pour-over with a motor, the Baratza Encore. For espresso ground by hand, the 1Zpresso J-Ultra.

This is a review summary. The S3 is the grinder I usually use; the other four I have not used. Each section below follows the maker's pages and published reviews, and most links go to Amazon.com search results (the S3 link goes to its product page), so compare sellers and check the price and stock on the day you buy.

## 1. TIMEMORE Chestnut S3 — the one I use

The hand grinder I usually use is the Timemore Chestnut S3. I grind about 30g at a time, for 2–3 cups. It is a hand grinder, so it is not for someone who wants to grind a large amount at once with a motor.

The burrs are S2C890 steel, 42 mm, cut on a 6-axis CNC. The adjustment is an external dial at 0.015 mm per click, 90 clicks per turn. The maker's starting ranges are espresso 1–15 clicks, pour-over 50–80, and French press 80–90. The handle folds down, the body is metal, and the base is silicone. The full specs, from the maker's US and Japanese pages, are in the [Chestnut S3 review](/en/articles/timemore-chestnut-s3/).

The Encore is the electric grinder reviewers have recommended longest for drip and pour-over. It uses 40 mm conical steel burrs with 40 grind settings, an 8 oz hopper, and a 5 oz grounds bin. It runs on a pulse switch or an on/off switch, and Baratza sells replacement parts, which matters for a grinder kept for years. The maker backs it with a 1-year warranty. It is built for filter brewing — drip, pour-over, French press, AeroPress — not for dialing in espresso.

## How to pick one

**Hand or electric?** A hand grinder takes no counter space and no outlet, and it is quiet. The cost is your time: about 30g takes a minute or two by hand. An electric grinder does a full pot at one press, and it is the only sensible choice for a 12-cup carafe.

**Espresso or filter?** Espresso needs fine steps you can return to — the J-Ultra's 8-micron clicks or the Opus's espresso range. Filter brewing is more forgiving: the Encore, the S3, and the OXO Brew all cover it. If you brew both, the Opus is the only electric pick here built for both ends.
