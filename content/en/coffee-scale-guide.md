The fastest way to make your pour-over taste the same every time is to weigh your coffee and water. This guide covers the features to look for in a coffee scale, and three scales to compare. It is a review summary. We have not used any of these scales. The facts below are from the makers' published specifications and this site's Japanese scale guides, checked November 23, 2026. ## How to weigh

Weigh the beans before grinding, not the grounds after. That keeps the dose the same every time. Put the server on the scale and pour into it, so the water weight shows as you pour. Start the timer when the pour starts. A starting ratio is 15g of coffee to 240g of water; adjust from there to taste.

## Six things to check

1. **Resolution**: 0.1g steps let you match your dose precisely. At minimum get 1g resolution; ideally 0.1g.
2. **Timer**: a built-in timer lets you watch weight and time together. Some start automatically when you begin pouring.
3. **Response speed**: does the display keep up as you pour? Check reviews for this.
4. **Size**: make sure you can still read the display with a dripper and server on top.
5. **Water resistance**: spills happen, so look for water resistance or at least an easy-to-wipe surface.
6. **Power**: AA/AAA batteries or USB rechargeable. For daily use, rechargeable is less hassle.

## 1. HARIO V60 Drip Scale VSTN-2000B — weight and time in one box

The VSTN-2000B weighs and times in one unit. The timer is started by hand and runs to 99 minutes 59 seconds. The scale reads 2 to 2,000g. The 0.1g step applies only while the total load is 2 to 200g; above 200g it reads 0.5g, and above 500g it reads 1g. So the 0.1g step is for the beans alone — with a dripper and server on the scale, the total is usually past 200g. The body is 120 × 190 × 29 mm, runs on two AAA batteries, and is ABS resin.

## 2. TIMEMORE Black Mirror Basic 2 — auto timer and flow rate

The Black Mirror Basic 2 reads 0.1g over 0.5 to 2,000g. Its auto timer starts when it detects water flowing: press the timer button once, wait for the 3-second countdown, and the timer starts on the first pour. If the brew runs longer than 40 seconds, the timer stops by itself when the dripper is lifted. A flow-rate display runs separately from the timer, so the pour speed stays visible while the timer runs. Power is a built-in 1,600 mAh lithium battery, charged over USB-C. It weighs in grams or ounces. TIMEMORE publishes no water-resistance rating for this scale, and there is no app.

## Rough budget tiers

- **Basic**: a scale with a timer. Plenty for a first scale.
- **Mid range**: faster response, water resistance, auto-start timers.
- **Premium**: flow-rate display, app connectivity, and more.

## How to pick one

**Manual timer and batteries?** The HARIO is the one with a hand-started timer and AAA batteries — no charging, nothing to plug in.

**Auto timer and rechargeable, without an app?** The TIMEMORE starts its timer on the first pour, shows flow rate, and charges over USB-C.

**Flow rate and an app?** The Pearl adds the app and the real-time flow-rate indicator, and it is the scale used at the World Barista Championship.
