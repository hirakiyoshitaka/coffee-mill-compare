When the usual beans taste bitter, the first thing to check is the grind size. The hand grinder I usually use is the Timemore Chestnut S3. I grind about 30g at a time, for 2–3 cups. It is a hand grinder, so it is not for someone who wants to grind a large amount at once with a motor. How fine or coarse to grind is in the [coffee grind size guide](/en/articles/coffee-grind-size-guide/).

## Specs from the maker

The figures below follow the official pages as checked on November 4, 2026: the [US product page](https://www.timemore.com/products/timemore-manual-coffee-grinder-chestnut-s3), the [Japanese product page](https://timemore.jp/products/timemore-s3-manual-coffee-grinder), and the comparison table on the [Chestnut S5 Pro official page](https://www.timemore.com/products/timemore-manual-coffee-grinder-chestnut-s5-pro).

- Burrs are S2C890. The US official page lists the material as SUS420, cut on a 6-axis CNC, hardness 58HRC
- Adjustment is an external dial. The US official page says 0.015mm per click. The S5 Pro comparison table lists the S3 as 0.015mm per click, 90 clicks per turn
- The burr gap at zero is 0.075mm, according to a note on the US official page. The same note says pressing the handle down can shift the shaft slightly and cause rubbing
- Bearings: the standard tolerance of 7μm was tightened to 5μm, according to the Japanese and US official pages
- The US official page gives starting click ranges: espresso 1–15, moka 5–20, pour-over 50–80, French press 80–90
- The handle folds down. The maker describes it as folding on a spring. The body is metal, and the base is silicone, according to the US official page
- A weight figure could not be confirmed in the body of the official product page on that day, so this page does not give one

## Grind settings by brew method

The starting click ranges below are the ones on Timemore's US official page (checked November 4, 2026), the same figures listed in the specs above. They are starting points, not a recipe: the beans and your taste decide the final setting. The dial moves 0.015mm per click, 90 clicks per turn.

| Brew method | Starting clicks |
|---|---|
| Espresso | 1–15 |
| Moka pot | 5–20 |
| Pour-over | 50–80 |
| French press | 80–90 |

For how each method's size looks and why it matters, see the [coffee grind size guide](/en/articles/coffee-grind-size-guide/).

## What I actually use it for

I grind about 30g at a time, for 2–3 cups, and brew pour-over most mornings. The beans are from [Coffee Carrot (珈琲きゃろっと)](/beans/coffee-carrot/), a subscription I kept for about a year. That shop page is in Japanese, and Coffee Carrot ships only within Japan, so for a US address treat this as a record of how the setup works and source beans locally.

The second grinder on this site is the [EPEIOS Essence Go](/en/articles/epeios-essence-go/). My one-line take on it: it just spins so easily. It is the one that comes apart into a carry case. I do not use the Chestnut S5 Pro; the [S5 Pro vs Essence Go comparison](/en/articles/timemore-s5-pro-vs-essence-go/) is a review summary. For a first hand grinder, see [how to choose](/en/articles/how-to-choose-hand-coffee-mill/).

## S3 vs 1Zpresso K-Ultra

We have not used the K-Ultra. The comparison below is a review summary from the official pages: the S3 figures are from Timemore's pages (checked November 4, 2026), and the K-Ultra figures are from 1Zpresso's pages (checked October 31, 2026, in the [1Zpresso lineup](/en/articles/1zpresso-hand-grinder-lineup/)).

| | Chestnut S3 | K-Ultra |
|---|---|---|
| Burr | S2C890 steel | Heptagonal burr (the product page did not give a burr diameter) |
| Adjustment | External dial, 0.015mm per click, 90 clicks per turn | External, 20 microns (0.02mm) per click, 100 clicks per turn |
| Capacity | About 30g at a time, for 2–3 cups | 35–40g |
| Weight | Not listed on the official product page | 700g |
| Job the maker assigns | Everyday pour-over at home | All brew methods |

The S3's click step is finer (0.015mm vs 0.02mm), and the K-Ultra holds a little more and is the one 1Zpresso positions across brew methods. If pour-over at home is the job, this site points to the S3 first — it is the one I usually use. For the full 1Zpresso list, see the [lineup](/en/articles/1zpresso-hand-grinder-lineup/).
