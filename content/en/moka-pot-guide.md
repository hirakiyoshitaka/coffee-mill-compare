A moka pot is a stovetop brewer. Water in the lower boiler heats up and pushes through a funnel of grounds into the upper chamber. Bialetti calls the result espresso-style. It is not espresso pressure, and this guide does not call it espresso.

**Quick pick:** gas or electric heat, classic octagonal aluminum — Moka Express. An induction hob with no adapter — Venus. A crema system on an induction hob — Brikka Induction.

This is a review summary. We have not used any moka pot. The facts below are from Bialetti's own pages, as recorded in this site's Japanese moka pot guide (pages opened November 1, 2026), and links go to Amazon.com search results, so compare sellers and check the price and stock on the day you buy.

## Read the cup size first

Bialetti's "cup" is an espresso cup of about 2 oz, not a mug. The maker's pages also list a total in ounces for each size, and the totals do not follow cup count times two — a 3-cup Moka Express is listed at 4.4 oz, not 6. Read the total ounces on the page, not the cup count alone. The same "6-cup" label means a different amount on another line: 9.1 oz on the Moka Express and 7.9 oz on the Venus.

## 1. Bialetti Moka Express — the octagonal classic

The Moka Express is the octagonal aluminum pot Bialetti has made since 1933. The body is aluminum, with a thermoplastic handle and knob. It works on gas and electric heat. On an induction hob it works only with Bialetti's induction plate — without the plate it is not on the maker's induction list.

The maker's US page lists six sizes, from a 1-cup pot at 2 oz to an 18-cup pot at 27.3 oz. Care is water only: rinse, no detergent, no dishwasher.

We have not used any Moka Express. The sizes, heat sources, and care above are from the maker's own pages.

## 2. Bialetti Venus — stainless steel for induction

The Venus is the stainless steel line: 18/10 stainless, and the maker's page says it goes on gas, electric, and induction with no adapter. That is the whole point of the line — if the hob is induction and no plate is wanted, this is the one the maker's page points to.

The sizes on the maker's page are 4, 6, and 10 cups, listed at 5.7, 7.9, and 15.5 oz. It is not the same size table as the Moka Express, so do not carry cup counts across lines.

We have not used any Venus. The material, heat sources, and sizes above are from the maker's own page.

## 3. Bialetti Brikka Induction — the crema system

The Brikka Induction is the line whose page explains a crema system, with a two-layer boiler of aluminum and steel. The maker's page lists one size, 4 cups, at about 5.4 oz of coffee and crema. Heat sources on the page are induction, gas, and electric.

## How to brew it, per the maker's page

The Italian Moka Express page gives the steps: room-temperature water up to the safety valve — above the valve the coffee comes out watery. A moka grind, fine but not too fine. Fill the funnel loosely, without pressing the grounds down. Low heat. When it starts gurgling, take it off the heat. Wash with water only, no detergent, no dishwasher. This site has no record of brewing with a moka pot.

Too fine a grind clogs the pot easily — a warning that shows up often on the brewer side. This is not this site's own test. Where the grind sits is in the [coffee grind size guide](/en/articles/coffee-grind-size-guide/): moka is on the fine side, one step coarser than espresso.

## How to pick one

**What heat?** On gas or electric, any of the three works. On induction with no adapter, the Venus is the line the maker's page lists. The Moka Express needs Bialetti's induction plate on induction.

**How much at once?** Read the total ounces, not the cup count. A 6-cup Moka Express (9.1 oz) and a 6-cup Venus (7.9 oz) are different amounts, and a "cup" is an espresso cup of about 2 oz, not a mug.

**Do you want crema?** The Brikka is the line whose page explains a crema system. The other two lines make the same stovetop style without that system.

**What grind?** Moka takes a fine grind, one step coarser than espresso. The hand grinder I usually use, the Timemore Chestnut S3, grinds about 30g at a time, for 2–3 cups. I have no record of brewing a moka pot with it. Grinding fine by hand every time is heavy work, so if moka is the main brew, the [electric grinder guide](/en/articles/electric-coffee-grinder-guide/) is worth a look too.
