Christmas is when many people buy their first espresso machine — for themselves, or as the gift for the coffee lover in the house. A machine bought in a hurry, in the middle of the sale season, is easy to get wrong: the wrong type for the way they actually drink coffee, a grinder question left unanswered, or a machine that does not fit the counter.

To be upfront: **we have not used the machines below.** Everything here comes from the makers' published specs and from reviews by outlets that tested them, and from our own comparison articles linked inline. This is a checklist, not a review — run through it before you order.

## 1. Decide who it is for

The same machine is a different buy depending on who unwraps it.

- **For yourself:** you can accept a learning curve. A hands-on machine that asks you to dose, tamp and steam milk yourself is fine, because you chose the ritual.
- **As a gift:** ease matters more than craft. The person opening it on Christmas morning has no practice, so a machine that guides the dose, froths milk on its own, or makes the drink at one touch is the safer gift. If they have to learn three skills before the first cup, the machine may sit unused.

If it is a gift and you are unsure which drinks they like, ask what they order at a café — espresso, latte, cold coffee — before you pick a type. That one answer narrows the choice more than any spec sheet.

## 2. Answer three questions before picking a model

**Espresso only, or other drinks too?** Some households drink espresso drinks and plain drip coffee, and iced coffee and cold brew as well. One machine can cover all of that — our [Ninja or Breville comparison](/en/articles/ninja-or-breville-first-espresso-machine/) shows a 3-in-1 machine that brews espresso, drip coffee and cold brew from one box. If everyone in the house drinks espresso only, the extra modes are bulk and buttons that never get used, and an espresso-only machine is the simpler thing on the counter.

**Milk drinks, or black coffee?** A latte drinker needs milk steaming; a black-coffee drinker does not. Manual steam wands give control and take practice. Hands-free milk presets and one-touch automatic milk give lattes with no technique. Our [milk frother guide](/en/articles/milk-frother-guide/) covers the separate-frother option if the machine you like has no milk system.

**Hands-on, or hands-off?** This is the real split. Semi-automatic machines use a portafilter you lock in by hand and ask you to judge the shot. Fully automatic bean-to-cup machines make the drink at one touch and hide the whole process. Our [Breville vs De'Longhi comparison](/en/articles/breville-vs-delonghi-all-in-one-espresso/) covers the one-touch side. There is no wrong answer — only a mismatch between the machine and the person using it.

## 3. Set the budget band first

Decide the band before looking at models, so a sale price cannot move you into a machine you did not want.

- **Under $1,000** buys a first machine with a built-in grinder — for example the Breville Barista Express Impress, which our [best all-in-one guide](/en/articles/best-all-in-one-espresso-machines-2026/) places in this band.
- **$1,000–$1,500** buys a one-touch bean-to-cup machine, such as the De'Longhi Rivelia in the same guide.
- **$1,500–$2,000** buys a one-touch machine with cold drinks included, such as the De'Longhi Eletta Explore, which brews cold brew as well as hot drinks.

These are bands, not prices — the price on the day moves with the sale. If a machine sits above the band you set, it is not a deal; it is a different budget.

## 4. The grinder question

Most first machines have a grinder built in, so there is no separate grinder to buy, place, or learn. That is the point of the all-in-one design: beans go in the hopper, and the machine grinds, doses and brews.

A separate grinder only enters the picture if you later want to brew pour-over or French press alongside espresso, or if you want finer control than the built-in grinder offers. Our [coffee grind size guide](/en/articles/coffee-grind-size-guide/) explains which grind each brew needs, and our [electric coffee grinder guide](/en/articles/electric-coffee-grinder-guide/) covers stand-alone grinders if you ever go that way.

One thing to settle before ordering: whole beans. A machine with a built-in grinder needs whole beans, not ground coffee — so have a bag of fresh whole beans ready for Christmas morning, or the first cup waits for a shopping trip.

## 5. Measure the counter

Espresso machines are deep and heavy — the two machines in our Ninja or Breville comparison weigh about 10.3 kg and about 11.7 kg. Measure the space under your cabinets before ordering, and check the depth, not just the width: the portafilter sticks out at the front, and the cup clearance decides which mugs fit.

Also check the voltage and region: buy the model for your country. The US models described here are 120 V; machines are not dual-voltage.

## 6. Buy during the sale, but check the price

The sale season is the reason to decide now rather than in January. Black Friday falls on Friday, November 27, 2026, and Cyber Monday on Monday, November 30, 2026; Amazon's Black Friday Week and Cyber Monday events run November 19–30, 2026. Our [Black Friday guide](/en/articles/amazon-black-friday-2026-coffee-gear/) has the full timing.

Two checks before you press buy:

- **Watch for a price raised just before the sale.** If the price went up in the weeks before Black Friday and then "fell" back to where it was, that is not a deal.
- **Check stock on the day.** A sale price on a machine that is out of stock helps no one, and popular models sell out during the event.

## 7. Know the return window before you give it

A Christmas gift that does not fit needs a return window that survives Christmas. For Amazon, most items bought between November 1 and December 31, 2026 can be returned through January 31, 2027 — so a machine bought in the sale and opened on Christmas Day can still go back in January. The usual return conditions still apply, and items that are normally non-returnable stay that way, so check the "Return policy" line on the product page before you order.

Keep the box until January. A machine returned without its packaging is a harder return than one returned in the box it came in.
