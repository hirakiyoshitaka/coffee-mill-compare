The easiest way to choose your first hand grinder is to look at fewer things. This guide covers just four: burr size, grind adjustment, weight, and whether it stays at home or travels. The numbers come from manufacturer specs; we didn't measure them ourselves.

Here's the bottom line first. If it's going to sit at home for daily pour-over, we recommend the **Timemore Chestnut S5 Pro**. If you want light turning or something that packs away, we recommend the **EPEIOS Essence Go**. I use both.

## 1. Burr size

Most hand grinders use cone-shaped (conical) burrs. Generally, bigger burrs grind more coffee per turn. That said, burr geometry and adjustment affect taste and speed too, so don't decide on diameter alone.

- Chestnut S5 Pro: 48mm
- EPEIOS Essence Go: 38mm (10-blade)

## 2. Grind adjustment

If you mostly brew pour-over, you rarely need ultra-fine steps. If you also want espresso-fine grounds, a grinder with smaller steps per click is easier to dial in.

- Chestnut S5 Pro: external dial, 90 clicks per turn, 15 microns per click (per the maker)
- EPEIOS Essence Go: 0–80 settings, read from the outside

An external dial lets you change the grind without taking the grinder apart, which makes life easier for beginners.

## 3. Weight

You hold a hand grinder in one hand while you turn it. Heavier grinders tend to feel more stable, but they're less fun to carry.

- Chestnut S5 Pro: about 910g
- EPEIOS Essence Go: about 870g

I use the green S5 Pro every day, mostly for pour-over, and the 910g has never bothered me.

## 4. Home or on the go?

If it lives on your counter, weight barely matters. If you want to take it out, check whether it comes apart and fits in a case. EPEIOS says the Essence Go disassembles into its own carry case.
