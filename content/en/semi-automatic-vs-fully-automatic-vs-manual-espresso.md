Espresso machines come in three types, and the type matters more than the brand. The real question is how much of the work you want to do yourself: pull a lever and control every second, lock in a portafilter and let a pump do the pressure, or press one button and get a latte.

To be upfront: **we have not used these machines.** Everything below comes from the makers' published specs and published guides, listed under Sources. Prices are bands, not list prices — check the store page for the current figure.

## Quick picks

- **Pick a manual lever machine if** you enjoy the process itself and want full control over pressure and timing. Example: Flair 58 Plus 2.
- **Pick a semi-automatic if** you want real espresso with a portafilter, and you are happy to grind, dose and tamp. Example: Breville Bambino.
- **Pick a fully automatic (bean-to-cup) if** you want café drinks at one touch and several people use the machine. Example: Philips 3200 LatteGo.

## The three types at a glance

| | Manual (lever) | Semi-automatic | Fully automatic (bean-to-cup) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pressure | Your hand on a lever | Electric pump | Electric pump |
| Grinding | You, separate grinder | You, separate or built-in | Inside the machine |
| Dose and tamp | You | You | Inside the machine |
| Shot timing | You | You start and stop it (some models stop on their own) | Machine |
| Milk | Separate frother | Steam wand, usually by hand | Usually automatic |
| Typical price range | $100–$800 | $200–$2,000 | $800–$5,000 |

The ranges above are the broad published ranges for each type, and they overlap. The per-model bands below are narrower. Labels are not standardised either: one brand's "automatic" may work like another brand's semi-automatic, so compare what you actually do at each step rather than trusting the label alone.

## 1. Manual — the lever, full control

A manual espresso machine has no electric pump. You raise a lever to draw water in and saturate the grounds (pre-infusion), then pull the lever down to create the pressure that pushes water through the coffee. The phrase "pulling a shot" comes from this motion. You control the pre-infusion time, the pressure and the flow for each shot — which is why enthusiasts like it, and why the result depends on your skill and can vary from shot to shot.

Most manual machines have no boiler and no milk system. You heat the water separately (some models add an electric preheat element), and you froth milk with a separate frother. You also need a separate grinder: a manual machine grinds nothing for you.

## 2. Semi-automatic — the portafilter, with a pump

A semi-automatic machine uses an electric pump to supply the pressure, and leaves the rest to you: you grind the beans, dose and tamp the grounds, lock in the portafilter, and start and stop the shot. Most home portafilter machines are semi-automatic, and so are most café machines. Some semi-automatics add programmed shot volumes that stop the pump for you; those are sold as "automatic" machines, but the workflow — grind, dose, tamp, lock in — is the same.

Milk is usually a steam wand you work by hand, although compact models automate the texturing. The grinder may be built in or separate: budget models have none, so budget for one.

## 3. Fully automatic — bean to cup at one touch

A fully automatic machine, also called super-automatic or bean-to-cup, grinds, doses, tamps, brews and often froths milk inside the machine. You put beans and water in, pick a drink on the screen, and the machine makes it. There is no portafilter for you to handle. The trade-off is control: you adjust strength, grind and volume within the model's programmed range, but you cannot change the extraction the way a lever or a portafilter lets you.

Milk comes from a carafe or an automatic frother, and the milk parts need rinsing after use — on the Philips LatteGo system the milk container comes apart in two pieces and goes in the dishwasher. The brew group is removable for cleaning on Philips and De'Longhi models.

## How to pick one

Ask these four questions in order:

1. **Who makes the coffee?** One person who enjoys the ritual points to manual or semi-automatic. Several people, or a household that wants the same drink with no practice, points to fully automatic.
2. **Do you enjoy the process, or just the drink?** If the making is part of the pleasure, a lever or a portafilter keeps it. If the making is a chore, a bean-to-cup removes it.
3. **How much milk?** Daily lattes and cappuccinos favour automatic milk — a fully automatic carafe, a Bambino Plus, or an assisted semi-automatic. Mostly black coffee makes the milk system a minor question.
4. **What does the whole setup cost?** A manual or a grinder-less semi-automatic needs a separate grinder, a tamper and a milk pitcher. One published example: a Bambino at $299.95 plus a Baratza Encore ESP at $199.95 comes to $499.90 as a complete setup. A fully automatic needs nothing extra to brew, but descaler and cleaning tablets are part of owning it.

Grind size is the other half of espresso, whatever type you pick.
