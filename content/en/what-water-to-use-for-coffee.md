A Reddit poster put the question plainly: he had heard that distilled water brings out more flavor than tap water, and wanted to know whether that was real or a myth — and whether the filtered water from the fridge could replace buying bottles of distilled water ([r/Coffee, 2018-03-11](https://www.reddit.com/r/Coffee/comments/83k4o9/filtered_water_vs_tap_vs_distilled_does_it_make_a/)).

It is a fair question, because water is the biggest ingredient in the cup. A brewed cup is about 98 percent water, so the water you choose does more to the flavor than most of the gear on the counter.

## Why distilled water alone falls flat

The replies were clear on one point: do not brew with distilled water on its own. Distilled water — and reverse-osmosis water — has almost no minerals in it, and minerals do part of the extraction work. Without them, the coffee comes out sharp, thin, and hollow. One commenter who tried all three side by side reported the same thing: tap water reflected how good the local water was, distilled water came out very sour and thin, and filtered water let the coffee itself come through cleanly.

The minerals that matter are calcium and magnesium, which help pull flavor out of the grounds, and alkalinity, which buffers the acidity so the cup does not taste harsh. The Specialty Coffee Association's water standard puts numbers on it: calcium hardness around 50 to 175 ppm, alkalinity around 40 to 70 ppm, pH near neutral, and no chlorine. Distilled water sits near zero on all of them, which is why it tastes empty even though it is pure.

That is the myth, sorted out: distilled water does not bring out more flavor. It takes the minerals away, and the flavor goes with them.

## Tap water depends on where you live

Tap water is a different story in every city. In the United States it often carries 200 to 250 ppm of minerals, above the ideal range of about 100 to 150 ppm, but the number alone does not decide it — the mix of minerals matters too, and so does chlorine.

The replies' rule of thumb is simple: if your tap water tastes good to drink on its own, it is usually fine for coffee. If it tastes bad on its own, it will not brew a good cup either. Taste the water plain first. That one check answers the question for most homes without buying anything.

## The practical answer: filtered tap

For most homes, the answer is tap water run through a carbon filter — a fridge filter or a filter pitcher. The filter takes out chlorine and off smells while leaving most of the minerals in place, which is exactly the balance the cup wants. It is also the easiest change to make: no bottles to buy, no packets to mix, just the water you already have, cleaned up.

If you want to go further, the replies named one more option: mineral packets such as Third Wave Water, added to distilled or reverse-osmosis water. That rebuilds the mineral balance from a blank base, and it is the route for brewers whose tap water is very hard or tastes off even after filtering. It is an option for later, not the first step — filtered tap is the first step.

Hard water has one more cost beyond flavor: scale. The minerals that make a cup taste dull also build up inside a brewer or kettle, so a machine that has never been descaled brews cooler and slower than it should. If the coffee went dull gradually while nothing else changed, cleaning is worth checking before buying anything.

## What not to buy

- **Bottles of distilled water for daily brewing.** Distilled water brews a thin cup on its own, and buying bottles every week is the most wasteful way to get there. Filtered tap does the job better.
- **A reverse-osmosis system just for coffee.** Reverse-osmosis water has the same problem as distilled water until minerals are added back. A filter pitcher solves the chlorine problem without stripping the water bare.
- **A new brewer to fix a water problem.** If the cup tastes off with every bean, the water is the suspect, not the brewer. Change the water first and taste the same coffee again.

## The question, answered

So does distilled water bring out more flavor than tap water? No — on its own it brews a thinner, sharper cup, because the minerals are part of the extraction. The poster's instinct was right: the filtered water from the fridge is the better answer. Start there. Taste the water plain, filter out the chlorine, and only reach for mineral packets if the tap water still tastes wrong after filtering.
