A moka pot brewer on Reddit put the hesitation plainly: the family electric grinder is set for drip, and the grounds they grind ahead and store go stale — "Should I purchase a manual hand grinder?" ([r/Coffee, January 27, 2025](https://www.reddit.com/r/Coffee/comments/1iaripp/should_i_purchase_a_manual_hand_grinder/))

The question underneath is not really "which grinder?" It is "is a grinder of my own worth it when the pot is already on the shelf?" The answer is yes — for this exact problem. A hand grinder gives you two things the shared setup cannot: your own grind setting, and grounds made right before you brew.

## Why the shared grinder is the problem

A moka pot takes a fine grind, one step coarser than espresso. A grinder set for drip grinds too coarse for it, and turning the shared grinder back and forth means losing the drip setting the family uses every time. Pre-grinding a batch ahead solves the setting problem but creates a new one: as the poster found, ground coffee goes stale in storage.

A hand grinder of your own breaks both links in that chain. You set it once for moka, and you grind right before brewing — no stale grounds, no fight over the family grinder's setting.

## One hand grinder, the moka pot you already have

Keep the pot. A hand grinder slots into the routine you have: weigh the beans, grind, and brew. The moka grind sits fine, on the finer side of granulated sugar — too fine and the pot clogs, so start at the fine end and adjust by taste. The [coffee grind size guide](/en/articles/coffee-grind-size-guide/) covers where moka sits next to espresso and pour-over, and the [moka pot guide](/en/articles/moka-pot-guide/) covers the brew itself.

## What you do not need yet

The rest of the set can come later, one piece at a time, when a specific annoyance shows up:

- **A new brewer:** the moka pot you have is the brewer. A hand grinder changes what goes into it, not the pot itself.
- **A second electric grinder:** one hand grinder covers the moka pot. The family grinder can stay on its drip setting.
- **A scale:** a kitchen scale works to start. A drip scale adds a timer — the [coffee scale guide](/en/articles/coffee-scale-guide/) covers what to weigh and when.

## The one honest warning

Grinding fine by hand, every time, is heavy work. If moka is the pot you brew every day, an electric grinder is worth a look too — the [electric grinder guide](/en/articles/electric-coffee-grinder-guide/) covers when a machine should do the grinding. A hand grinder is the right first step when the problem is the shared setting and stale grounds; it is not the only answer when fine grinding is the whole daily routine.

## The question, answered

So is a manual hand grinder worth buying for a moka pot? If the family grinder stays on drip and your grounds go stale in the jar, yes — one hand grinder, your own setting, ground fresh right before brewing, is the fix. Buy that one thing, keep the pot you have, and let the rest of the set wait.
