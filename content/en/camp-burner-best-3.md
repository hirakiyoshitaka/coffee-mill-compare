A camp stove is less about the biggest kilowatt number and more about what you can carry and what pan it holds. One pot on your back, a wider base for a medium pan, or a tabletop unit that deals with wind beside the car: those are three different jobs.

We have not used these three stoves. There is no hands-on note. Figures come from maker pages and the instruction manual, checked on 2 November 2026. The order follows solo carry versus a car-side table, not commission. Prices are guides. Canisters are sold separately for all three.

Do not use any of them inside a tent or a vehicle. SOTO marks both stoves for outdoor use only. Iwatani’s product page tells you never to use the Tough Maru in a tent or in a car.

## SOTO Regulator Stove ST-310

Open this one first for solo camping. In use, the body is 166 mm wide, 142 mm deep, and 110 mm high. Stored, it is 140 × 70 × 110 mm. Weight is 330 g for the body only. The burner, control knob, and trivet are stainless steel. The canister holder and ignition switch are resin. It is made in Japan.

Output is 2.9 kW (2,500 kcal/h) on either an ST-760 or an ST-700 canister. Burn time is about 1.5 hours on one of those canisters. The maker calculates output from five minutes of burning at 25°C with no wind, and burn time from 30 minutes of data in the same conditions. Ignition is piezo. A stuff sack is included. The load rating is 5 kg. The trivet is 130 mm outside and 47 mm inside. The micro regulator is described as holding output steady from 5°C to 25°C. Fuel is a SOTO dedicated cassette canister, sold separately. Outdoor use only.

**Review summary (not first-hand use).** The checks that keep coming up are the separate canister, the no-wind test condition, and whether a small pot sits in the 47 mm inner opening. No star rating is copied here.

## SOTO Regulator Stove Range ST-340

Same outline as the ST-310, with a wider burner head. In use it is 166 × 142 × 110 mm, stored 140 × 70 × 110 mm, and the body weighs 360 g. Output is 3.3 kW (2,800 kcal/h) on ST-760 or ST-700, for about 1.4 hours on one canister. The test-condition notes match the ST-310.

The burner head is 66 mm across, which the maker says is 21 mm larger than the ST-310, and it is positioned for cookware bottoms from 11 to 19 cm. The trivet diameter is 130 mm. An ignition assist lever is standard. Load rating 5 kg, piezo ignition, stuff sack included, outdoor only, canister sold separately. The product page showed ¥9,790 including tax.

Against the ST-310, the published gaps are 30 g, 0.4 kW, about 0.1 hour of burn time, and the wider head. Stay with rank 1 for a small solo pot. Open rank 2 when the pan bottom is past 11 cm and you want the heat spread out.

**Review summary (not first-hand use).** The point of comparison is the wider head for medium pans, and the extra 30 g.

## Iwatani Cassette Fu Tough Maru (CB-ODX-1)

This is not a pack stove. It is a tabletop unit that rides in the car. The model is CB-ODX-1, in black (CB-ODX-1-BK) and olive (CB-ODX-1-OL). Maximum output is 3.3 kW (2,800 kcal/h). The body is 341 × 283 × 129 mm. The case is 376 × 341 × 136 mm. Body weight is about 2.4 kg, and about 3.5 kg in the case. The suggested retail price is ¥11,000 including tax (¥10,000 before tax).

Gas use is about 236 g/h, and continuous burn is about 75 minutes. Both notes use 20–25°C. Consumption is 30 minutes of use scaled to one hour. Continuous time is a full-power measurement that empties one new canister. Ignition is piezo. Safety devices include a pressure sensor that releases the canister. The canister attaches by magnet. Fuel is Iwatani cassette gas or Iwatani Cassette Gas Power Gold, not a SOTO canister. The manual allows pan bottoms up to 24 cm, at least 16 cm for small pans, and a total weight including food of 20 kg or less. A carrying case is included. Made in Japan.

The maker describes a double windbreak that blocks wind and still lets combustion air through, and says strong wind can still make it unusable. The product page says never to use it in a tent or in a car.

The smaller Tough Maru Jr. (CB-ODX-JR) is a different model, with different output and weight, so it is not on this list.

**Review summary (not first-hand use).** The checks are the 2.4 kg weight, the windbreak, the 20 kg load, and the fact that SOTO canisters do not fit.
