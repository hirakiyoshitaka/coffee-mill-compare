A French press brewer on Reddit put the hesitation plainly: after switching from a drip machine to a French press, the pre-ground coffee tasted bad, and the price of an electric burr grinder gave them pause — "Should I Get a Hand Grinder for my French Press?" ([r/Coffee, February 20, 2015](https://www.reddit.com/r/Coffee/comments/2wio5d/should_i_get_a_hand_grinder_for_my_french_press/))

The question underneath is not really "which grinder?" It is "is a hand grinder enough when I brew one cup at a time?" For a French press, the answer is yes — and the press is the kindest place to start grinding by hand. A hand grinder gives you the two things the pre-ground bag cannot: a coarse grind that fits the press, and grounds made right before you brew.

## Why the pre-ground bag is the problem

A French press brews by immersion: coarse grounds sit in hot water for about 4 minutes, and a metal mesh holds the grounds back at the end. That mesh lets fine particles through, so a grind that is too fine leaves silt in the cup. Pre-ground coffee is usually ground for a drip machine — too fine for a press — and it goes stale in storage besides. So the cup tastes muddy and flat at the same time, and no change of beans fixes it, because the grind is the problem.

A hand grinder of your own breaks both links in that chain. You set it coarse, once, and you grind right before brewing — no stale grounds, no silt from a drip grind.

## One hand grinder, the press you already have

Keep the press. A hand grinder slots into the routine you have: weigh the beans, grind coarse, and brew. The French press grind sits at the coarse end — often compared to raw sugar — and the [coffee grind size guide](/en/articles/coffee-grind-size-guide/) shows where it sits next to espresso, moka, and pour-over. The [French press vs AeroPress guide](/en/articles/french-press-vs-aeropress/) covers the brew itself: about 4 minutes of steeping, water at 92–96 °C, and a mesh that needs a coarse grind to do its job.

The hand grinder I usually use is the Timemore Chestnut S3. I grind about 30g at a time, for 2–3 cups. The maker's page lists a French press range for it — 80–90 clicks — but I have no record of brewing a French press with it, so treat that range as a starting point and adjust by taste. If a lot of silt stays in the cup, go coarser. The full review, with its official specs, is in the [Chestnut S3 review](/en/articles/timemore-chestnut-s3/).

The poster also asked about the water-to-coffee ratio. This site gives no fixed ratio for a French press, and a number copied from elsewhere would be a guess dressed as a fact. What does work is weighing: a [coffee scale](/en/articles/coffee-scale-guide/) lets you use the same beans and the same water each time, and then adjust by taste — more beans if the cup tastes thin, less if it tastes heavy. A kitchen scale works to start.

## What you do not need yet

The rest of the set can come later, one piece at a time, when a specific annoyance shows up:

- **A new brewer:** the French press you have is the brewer. A hand grinder changes what goes into it, not the press itself.
- **A second electric grinder:** one hand grinder covers the press. The poster already planned to buy a Baratza Encore later, as an upgrade — a hand grinder is the stopgap that makes the press drinkable until then, not a replacement for that plan.
- **A scale:** a kitchen scale works to start. A drip scale adds a timer — the [coffee scale guide](/en/articles/coffee-scale-guide/) covers what to weigh and when.

## The one honest warning

A coarse grind is light work by hand — the French press is the easiest brew method to grind for, because the dial sits at the coarse end and the beans break apart with little resistance. The warning runs the other way: if you later switch to espresso, the same grinder has to work much harder at the fine end, and that is when an electric grinder earns its place. The [electric grinder guide](/en/articles/electric-coffee-grinder-guide/) covers when a machine should do the grinding. For a French press and one cup at a time, a hand grinder is the right first step.

## The question, answered

So is a hand grinder worth buying for a French press? If pre-ground coffee tastes bad and an electric grinder feels like too much to spend on one cup at a time, yes — one hand grinder, set coarse, ground fresh right before brewing, is the fix. Buy that one thing, keep the press you have, and let the rest of the set wait.
