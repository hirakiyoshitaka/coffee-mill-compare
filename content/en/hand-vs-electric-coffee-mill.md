The first question when buying a coffee grinder is usually "hand or electric?" My first grinder was a manual one, the [Timemore Chestnut S5 Pro](/en/articles/timemore-chestnut-s5-pro/), and I use it for pour-over every day. I don't own an electric grinder, so I'll describe that side in general terms only.

## The differences at a glance

| | Hand grinder | Electric grinder |
|---|---|---|
| Effort | Tens of seconds of cranking per cup | Push a button |
| Noise | Quiet | Motor noise |
| Power | None needed | Needs an outlet |
| Portability | Many models travel well | Mostly stays put |
| Price (models with even grinds) | Roughly ¥10,000–¥30,000+ in Japan | From around ¥10,000 in Japan |
| Best for | 1–2 cups | Whole household, batch grinding |

## A hand grinder suits you if you

- Brew one or two cups, just for yourself
- Want to grind early in the morning or late at night without waking anyone
- Enjoy the ritual of grinding as part of the brew
- Want to take it camping or to the office

In my experience, the S5 Pro's roughly 910g weight is a non-issue at home. If you want the lightest possible turning, the [EPEIOS Essence Go](/en/articles/epeios-essence-go/) is worth a look; my take on it is that it just spins so easily. The two are compared in [Timemore S5 Pro vs EPEIOS Essence Go](/en/articles/timemore-s5-pro-vs-essence-go/).

## An electric grinder suits you if you

- Grind for the whole household at once
- Want to cut your morning routine to the minimum
- Want to avoid strain on your wrists or arms

For how to pick one, see our [electric coffee grinder guide](/en/articles/electric-coffee-grinder-guide/).

## Still undecided?

If you brew one or two cups of pour-over a day, I'd start with a hand grinder. That's how I started.
