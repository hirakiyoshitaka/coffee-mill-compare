The first question when buying a coffee grinder is usually "hand or electric?" The hand grinder I usually use is the Timemore Chestnut S3. I grind about 30g at a time, for 2–3 cups. I do not use the Chestnut S5 Pro. I don't own an electric grinder, so I'll describe that side in general terms only.

## The differences at a glance

| | Hand grinder | Electric grinder |
|---|---|---|
| Effort | Tens of seconds of cranking per cup | Push a button |
| Noise | Quiet | Motor noise |
| Power | None needed | Needs an outlet |
| Portability | Many models travel well | Mostly stays put |
| Price (models with even grinds) | Roughly ¥10,000–¥30,000+ in Japan | From around ¥10,000 in Japan |
| Best for | 1–2 cups | Whole household, batch grinding |

The table's hand-grinder prices are for models with an even grind, roughly ¥10,000–¥30,000+ in Japan. The models listed on this site cost over about ¥10,000 as a guide. Prices change.

## Grind quality: burrs on both sides

A hand grinder in this price range grinds with burrs, and so does a good electric grinder. The difference on the electric side is the type. A blade grinder chops the beans with a spinning blade, so the grind comes out uneven and you judge coarseness by how long you run it. A flat burr grinder gives a more even grind, and a conical burr grinder turns more slowly and is said to generate less heat. Fines — the fine dust in the grind — can make coffee taste muddy, so check whether the maker addresses them and what reviews say. The full criteria are in our [electric coffee grinder guide](/en/articles/electric-coffee-grinder-guide/).

## How long does hand grinding take?

A hand grinder takes tens of seconds of cranking per cup — that is the real cost of choosing one. I usually grind about 30g at a time, for 2–3 cups, with the Chestnut S3. An electric grinder does the same job at the push of a button. If your morning is short and grinding is one step among many, that difference matters more than the price gap. If grinding is part of the ritual you enjoy, it does not.

## Noise, and when you grind

A hand grinder is quiet — no motor, no power cord — which is why it suits early mornings and late nights, when you want to grind without waking anyone. An electric grinder makes motor noise, and that matters most if you grind early, while others are asleep, or in a thin-walled room. If you grind after everyone is up, noise drops out of the decision.

## What if you want espresso?

Espresso needs a fine grind, and grinding that fine by hand is the hardest case for a hand grinder — more turns, more effort. Before choosing, read the [coffee grind size guide](/en/articles/coffee-grind-size-guide/) on whether to grind espresso-fine by hand at all. If espresso is your main brew, an electric grinder with fine-grained adjustment, or a machine with a built-in grinder, is the easier path.

## A hand grinder suits you if you

- Brew one or two cups, just for yourself
- Want to grind early in the morning or late at night without waking anyone
- Enjoy the ritual of grinding as part of the brew
- Want to take it camping or to the office

About 910g is the S5 Pro's official weight, not a note from use. I do not use the Chestnut S5 Pro. If you want the lightest possible turning, the [EPEIOS Essence Go](/en/articles/epeios-essence-go/) is worth a look; my take on it is that it just spins so easily. The two are compared in [Timemore S5 Pro vs EPEIOS Essence Go](/en/articles/timemore-s5-pro-vs-essence-go/).

## An electric grinder suits you if you

- Grind for the whole household at once
- Want to cut your morning routine to the minimum
- Want to avoid strain on your wrists or arms

For how to pick one, see our [electric coffee grinder guide](/en/articles/electric-coffee-grinder-guide/).

## Still undecided?

If you brew one or two cups of pour-over a day, I'd start with a hand grinder. That's how I started. Three questions settle the rest: how many cups at once — one or two points to a hand grinder, a whole household points to electric; when you grind — early or late points to the quiet one; and whether grinding itself is part of the fun — if it is, a hand grinder, and if it is a chore to shorten, electric.
