If your first espresso machine comes down to two names, Ninja and Breville are the pair most people land on. Ninja's Luxe Café Premier (ES601) is the newcomer that does espresso, drip coffee and cold brew from one box. Breville's Barista Express Impress is the current version of the machine that has defined the first-espresso-machine category for years.

To be upfront: **we have not used either machine.** Everything below comes from the makers' published specs and from reviews by outlets that tested them (linked inline). The question is not which machine is "better" in general, but which one fits the way you will actually make coffee.

## The short answer

**Pick the Ninja Luxe Café Premier if** you want the easiest start and the most drinks from one machine. It suggests the grind, doses by weight, and froths milk hands-free — and it also brews drip coffee and cold brew, so it can replace more than an espresso machine.

**Pick the Breville Barista Express Impress if** you want to learn espresso properly and you mainly drink espresso-based drinks. You steam the milk yourself, you see the puck, and the machine coaches you on the dose — but it does one job, espresso, and leaves drip coffee to another machine.

## What the Ninja does differently

The Luxe Café Premier is a 3-in-1 machine: espresso (double or quad shot), drip coffee in classic, rich or over-ice styles in sizes from 6 to 18 oz, and cold brew — all from whole beans ground by the built-in grinder. Two features set it apart from most grinder-and-portafilter machines:

- **Weight-based dosing.** A built-in scale weighs the dose for each drink, so you are not judging it by eye or by grind time.
- **Barista Assist Technology.** The machine recommends a grind size for the drink you choose — finer for espresso, coarser for drip or cold brew — and adjusts the brewing for it.

Milk is hands-free: the Dual Froth System offers four presets (steamed milk, thin froth, thick froth, cold foam) with a steam wand and a whisk, so lattes and iced drinks need no steaming technique. An assisted tamper is included — you still tamp by hand; the step-up model, the Luxe Café Pro (ES701), adds a built-in tamping arm and a hot water dispenser.

## What the Breville does differently

The Barista Express Impress is the hands-on classic, modernised. Its assisted Impress tamp removes the biggest beginner variable — it tamps for you and tells you whether to adjust the dose — while keeping the craft: you still lock in the 54 mm portafilter, watch the shot, and steam the milk yourself on a manual wand. PID temperature control keeps the water steady, and at about 10.3 kg it is the lighter of the two.

What it does not do: no touchscreen guidance, no automatic milk, no cold drinks. It is an espresso machine, and it expects you to enjoy the process of making espresso.

## What reviewers say

- **TechRadar** (Ninja Luxe Café review) put the Ninja ahead of the Breville Barista Express it was compared with, mainly for the foolproof system and the drip-coffee bonus, and called it "perfect for novices" — while flagging that it is bulky, has no hot water line, and that the steam wand cannot be used manually.
- **Tom's Guide** (Ninja Luxe Café review) called it the best espresso machine for beginners you can buy today, and found every drink it made came out near-perfect, from classic espresso shots to cold brew with cold foam. Its comparison with the Pro notes the trade-off: the original ES601 needs hand-tamping, while the Pro's tamping arm makes the workflow simpler.
- **On the Breville side**, our [Breville vs De'Longhi comparison](/en/articles/breville-vs-delonghi-all-in-one-espresso/) records what reviewers praise in the Barista Express Impress: the assisted tamp takes the guesswork out of dosing while keeping the craft, and the manual wand is the part people who enjoy steaming milk actually want.

The pattern across the reviews is consistent: the Ninja removes more steps, the Breville keeps more of the ritual. Neither review calls the other machine a bad buy.

## Portafilter vs automatic: which is easier at home?

Both of these machines still use a portafilter you lock in by hand — neither is a one-touch bean-to-cup machine that makes the drink from a picture on a screen. The difference is how much of the work around the portafilter is automated:

- **Ninja** automates the most: grind suggestion, weight-based dosing, and hands-free milk presets. You lock in the portafilter, press a button, and the machine does the rest of the routine.
- **Breville Barista Express Impress** automates the tamp and coaches the dose, but you choose the grind, judge the shot, and steam the milk yourself.

If you want zero portafilter at all, that is a different category — the fully automatic bean-to-cup machines in our [Breville vs De'Longhi comparison](/en/articles/breville-vs-delonghi-all-in-one-espresso/) press a picture and the drink comes out. The trade-off is the ritual itself: with a bean-to-cup machine you never see the puck, and you never learn what a fast or slow shot means.

## Drip + espresso combo machines: who they suit

The Ninja is the combo machine here, and the combo is the whole point of it. It suits you if:

- Your household drinks both espresso drinks and plain drip coffee, and you want one machine on the counter instead of two
- You drink iced coffee and cold brew in summer as well as hot drinks in winter
- You want one box to cover guests who drink different things

It does not suit you if everyone in the house drinks espresso only — then the drip and cold-brew modes are bulk and buttons you never use, and the espresso-only Breville is the simpler machine on the counter. One limit to know: drip and cold brew top out at 18 oz at a time, so it does not brew a big pot for a crowd.

## Do you need a grinder to start?

No — not with either of these machines. Both have a built-in conical burr grinder with 25 settings, so there is no separate grinder to buy, place, or learn. That is the point of the all-in-one design: beans go in the hopper, and the machine grinds, doses and brews.

A separate grinder only enters the picture if you later want to brew pour-over or French press alongside espresso, or if you want finer control than the built-in grinder offers. ## If you want more guidance or more drinks

- **More guidance:** the [Breville Barista Touch Impress](/en/articles/breville-vs-delonghi-all-in-one-espresso/) adds a touchscreen that walks you through grinding, tamping, extraction and automatic milk, plus cold extraction for iced drinks.
- **More automation:** the [Breville Oracle Jet](/en/articles/breville-vs-delonghi-all-in-one-espresso/) grinds, doses and tamps automatically and steams milk hands-free, with a commercial-size 58 mm portafilter.
- **More from Ninja:** the Luxe Café Pro (ES701) adds the built-in tamping arm, a hot water dispenser for Americanos, a single basket, and ristretto and lungo settings. Tom's Guide judged the extra functions worth considering, but not a must-have if the original already covers your drinks.
