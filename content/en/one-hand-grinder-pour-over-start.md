A pour-over lover on Reddit put the hesitation plainly: they love the V60 most, and they wonder whether a better hand grinder is worth buying — "But I'm wondering if anyone has owned this previously, 'upgraded,' and could share what is better than this grinder." ([r/Coffee, July 18, 2026](https://www.reddit.com/r/Coffee/comments/1uzdskz/hand_grinder_comparison_timemore_c3esp/))

The question underneath is not really "which grinder?" It is "do I have to buy the whole set at once?" The answer is no. The grinder is the one purchase that changes the cup. Start with one hand grinder and the dripper you already have.

## Why the grinder comes first

A dripper shapes the water's path, but the grind decides how the coffee extracts. Pre-ground coffee goes stale, and a blade grinder chops unevenly, so some grounds over-extract while others under-extract. A burr hand grinder fixes both: you grind fresh, and the grind size stays even. That is the whole upgrade — everything else on the counter can wait.

## What you do not need yet

The rest of the set can come later, one piece at a time, when a specific annoyance shows up:

- **Kettle:** any kettle that pours thin enough works to start. A gooseneck helps control where the water lands — the [gooseneck kettle guide](/en/articles/gooseneck-kettle-guide/) covers spout, temperature control, capacity, and heat source.
- **Scale:** a kitchen scale works to start. A drip scale adds a timer — the [coffee scale guide](/en/articles/coffee-scale-guide/) covers what to weigh and when.
- **Second grinder:** one hand grinder covers pour-over. An electric grinder only earns its place when you grind for a whole household or switch between espresso and filter every day.

## The upgrade question, answered

So is upgrading the hand grinder worth it? If your current grinder already grinds evenly for the V60, a new one changes little — spend the money on beans instead. If the grind comes out uneven, or grinding feels like a chore you avoid, that is the upgrade that changes the cup. Buy that one thing, keep the dripper you love, and let the rest of the set wait.
