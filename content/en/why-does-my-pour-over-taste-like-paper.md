A Reddit poster put the frustration plainly: even after washing the filter twice, the pour over still tastes papery and slightly soapy — "Making a pot of pour over coffee and it still tastes papery even after washing the filter." ([r/Coffee, 2018-03-27](https://www.reddit.com/r/Coffee/comments/87ivra/making_a_pot_of_pour_over_coffee_and_it_still/))

The setup is a Chemex brewer with No. 4 compostable unbleached filters, no particular brand. The question underneath is not really "how do I rinse better?" It is "is there a better filter to buy?" The answer, from the replies, is that the filter itself is the flavour here — and the fix starts with changing the paper, not the brewer.

## The filter is the flavour here

A paper filter is cellulose pulp — pressed paper — and dry paper gives off loose fibres and papery compounds when hot water first hits it. Unbleached brown filters carry more of that note than bleached white ones, and Chemex filters are among the thickest paper available, so there is more paper in the water's path. That is why two rinses did not fix the poster's cup: the rinse washes the loose compounds out, but thick unbleached paper keeps giving off its flavour.

This is also why the note shows up most in light roasts. A dark roast has enough of its own roasty flavour to cover the paper; a light roast leaves the papery note with nowhere to hide. If the cup tastes like cardboard in the finish, dry and faintly fibrous after the swallow, the filter is the first suspect — not the beans.

## First fix: switch to bleached white filters

The top replies agreed on one thing: get the regular white ones. Bleached filters, and the Chemex brand bleached filters in particular, carry far less papery flavour than the unbleached ones. The brewer stays the same; only the paper changes.

Oxygen-bleached white filters are the usual pick. The bleaching does not add flavour to the cup — it removes the compounds that make brown paper taste like paper. If the current box is unbleached and the cup tastes papery even after rinsing, the next box should be white.

## Second fix: rinse properly, with hot water

A rinse still matters, even with white filters. Pour just-boiled water through the empty filter in a slow spiral, covering all the paper, and let the water sit in the filter for a moment before dumping it. The rinse does three jobs at once: it washes the loose paper compounds out before any coffee touches the paper, it preheats the brewer so the coffee stays hot, and it seats the filter flat against the dripper walls so the water flows evenly.

Dump the rinse water into the sink, not into the cup. The water that comes through a dry filter is the papery water — that is the whole point of the rinse. Our [beginner hand drip recipe](/en/articles/beginner-hand-drip-recipe/) does the same step: Hario says to rinse the filter so the paper smell comes off.

## Pour onto the coffee, not the paper

Once the coffee is in, aim the pour at the coffee bed, not at the bare paper. Water that hits the side of the filter slides straight down the wall without touching the grounds, and it picks up paper flavour on the way. Keeping the stream on the grounds keeps the paper out of the recipe as much as possible.

The same rule applies to the rinse: pour over the paper, then throw that water away in a separate mug or the sink, so none of it ends up in the brewed coffee.

## The soapy note is a different clue

The poster also tasted something slightly soapy, and that note points somewhere else: the water. Very hard or mineral-heavy water can taste soapy, and so can soap residue left in the server or mug. If the papery note goes away with white filters but a soapy edge stays, brew the same coffee with filtered water and a clean, well-rinsed server, and see which one clears it.

## If the paper still wins

For a very sensitive palate, the replies offered one more direction: a metal filter. Metal lets the coffee oils through, so the cup is heavier and less clean-tasting than a paper-filtered cup — a trade-off, not a free fix. Another suggestion was a different paper altogether, such as bamboo-fibre Melitta-style filters. Both are directions to try after the white filters, not instead of them.

## What not to buy

- **A new brewer to fix a papery cup.** The brewer is fine. The paper is the problem, and a new dripper with the same unbleached filters tastes the same.
- **A big pack of the same unbleached filters.** Buying more of the thing causing the taste does not fix it. Finish the box you have, or switch, but do not stock up on the paper that tastes like paper.
- **Darker beans to cover the paper note.** A darker roast adds its own bitter edge. It hides nothing, and the papery finish is still there underneath.

## The question, answered

So why does the pour over still taste papery after two rinses? Most likely the filter: thick unbleached paper keeps giving off its flavour no matter how many times it is rinsed. Switch to bleached white filters — the Chemex brand bleached ones were the replies' pick — rinse with hot water and dump it, and pour onto the coffee, not the paper. If a soapy note stays after that, check the water next.
