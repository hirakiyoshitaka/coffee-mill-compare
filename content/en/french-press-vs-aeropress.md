A French press and an AeroPress both brew by immersion: the grounds sit in the water while the coffee steeps. The difference is what happens at the end. A French press pushes a metal mesh down through the coffee to hold the grounds back. An AeroPress pushes the coffee through a paper filter with a plunger. That one difference changes the body of the cup, how much you can brew at once, and how you clean up.

**Quick pick:** for two or more cups at the table, a French press. For one clean cup, or coffee on a trip, an AeroPress.

This is a review summary. We have not used either brewer. Each section below follows the makers' pages and published guides, and links go to Amazon.com search results, so compare sellers and check the price and stock on the day you buy.

## The two at a glance

|  | French press | AeroPress |
|---|---|---|
| Filter | Metal mesh, no paper | Paper microfilter (a metal disc fits too) |
| Brew time | About 4 minutes of steeping | 1–2 minutes |
| Grind | Coarse | Medium-fine |
| Cup | Full body, oils stay in | Cleaner, lighter body |
| Capacity | Serves 2–4 cups | One cup at a time |
| Cleanup | Scoop the grounds, rinse the mesh | Push the puck out, rinse |
| Travel | Glass needs care on the road | Plastic, and the Go version packs into its own mug |

## 1. French press — the fullest cup at the table

A French press is a beaker with a plunger. Coarse grounds and hot water go in together, the coffee steeps, and the mesh is pressed down to separate the grounds from the drink. There is no paper filter, so the oils stay in the cup and the body comes out full. The trade-off is sediment: the mesh lets fine particles through, so a grind that is too fine leaves silt in the cup.

The published numbers from the makers' pages are a 4-minute steep — Bodum's page for the Chambord gives 92–96 °C water and 4 minutes — and a coarse grind. Sizes run from a 300 mL press for 2 cups to a 600 mL press for 4 cups (HARIO's Cafe Press U), and Bodum's Chambord is listed at 34 oz. Glass bodies are the usual shape, with stainless frames and lids on some models.

A press suits a slow morning for two or more people: one steep makes the whole pot. It is the wrong pick for a single cup on a rushed morning, when the same 4 minutes and the same cleanup serve one mug.

We have not used any French press. The sizes, temperatures, and times above are from the makers' own pages, as recorded in this site's Japanese French press guide.

## 2. AeroPress — one clean cup, fast

The AeroPress is a plastic chamber, a plunger, and a cap that holds a small round paper filter. It was invented in 2005 by Alan Adler. Grounds and water steep in the chamber, then a gentle push on the plunger forces the coffee through the filter into the cup. The push is light pressure, far below espresso pressure, but it is enough to move the coffee through paper quickly — which is why the AeroPress can use a finer grind than a French press and still finish in 1–2 minutes.

The maker's recipe is 12–14 g of medium-fine coffee, water at about 85 °C, a short stir, a 30-second steep, and a gentle press until it hisses. The box includes 350 paper microfilters. The paper catches the fine particles and most of the oils, so the cup comes out clean and bright, with less body than a press. A metal disc filter fits the same cap for a fuller cup. One brew makes a single cup, about 200–250 mL.

There is also an inverted method: the brewer is flipped so the chamber sits on the plunger, the coffee steeps without dripping, and it is turned over to press. For travel, the AeroPress Go is a compact version whose chamber, plunger, scoop, stirrer, and filters nest inside its own lidded mug.

## How to pick one

**How many cups?** A press brews for the table in one steep. An AeroPress brews one cup, so a second person means a second brew.

**What kind of cup?** If you want a full, heavy cup with the oils in it, the press's mesh gives you that. If you want a clean cup where lighter flavors show, the AeroPress's paper gives you that.

**How much time?** The AeroPress finishes in 1–2 minutes; the press needs about 4 minutes of steeping plus the time to scoop and rinse the mesh. The AeroPress also cleans itself as you push the puck out.

**Will it travel?** The AeroPress Go packs into its own mug and is plastic. A glass press is a table brewer — it needs care on the road.

**What grind?** French press takes a coarse grind; the AeroPress takes medium-fine. Where to set the grind for each brew method is in the [coffee grind size guide](/en/articles/coffee-grind-size-guide/). On the hand grinder I usually use, the Timemore Chestnut S3, the maker's starting range for French press is 80–90 clicks — the coarse end of the dial. I grind about 30g at a time, for 2–3 cups.
