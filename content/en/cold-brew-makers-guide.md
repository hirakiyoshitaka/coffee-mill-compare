Cold brew is coffee steeped in cold water instead of brewed with hot water. Put coarse grounds and cold water in the maker, leave it in the fridge overnight, and take the filter out in the morning. There is no pouring technique to learn, and cold brew is often described as smooth and mellow.

## Two ways to make cold brew

| Type | How it works | Check before buying |
|---|---|---|
| Immersion pitcher | Grounds steep in cold water in a jar, then a mesh filter lifts out | Filter fineness and capacity |
| Slow-drip tower | Cold water drips through the grounds a drop at a time | Counter space and drip speed |

An immersion pitcher is the simpler shape: grounds and water go in together, and the filter keeps the grounds out of the cup. A slow-drip tower takes more space and more watching, and it is the shape cafes often use.

## Six things to check

1. **Capacity**: A 1-liter pitcher makes several glasses. Check whether the maker brews ready-to-drink coffee or a concentrate you dilute with water or milk — a concentrate makes more servings from the same pitcher.
2. **Filter**: A fine mesh filter keeps silt out of the cup. Check whether the filter lifts out in one piece and whether it is easy to rinse clean.
3. **Grind**: Cold brew takes the coarsest grind on the chart — the same as a French press, or coarser still. Too fine, and the brew can come out overly strong. The [grind size guide](/en/articles/coffee-grind-size-guide/) puts cold brew at the coarse end.
4. **Material**: Glass does not hold odors from batch to batch. Plastic is lighter and harder to break, which matters if the pitcher travels to the fridge and back often.
5. **Fridge fit**: A tall pitcher needs a shelf with headroom. Measure the shelf before you buy.
6. **Cleaning**: Grounds stick to mesh. Check that the filter and the jar come apart and rinse clean without a brush reaching into a narrow neck.
