## How to choose a golf rangefinder starts with laser versus GPS

A golf rangefinder is a way of measuring before it is a model name.

A laser rangefinder puts a beam on a target you can see and returns the distance to that target. Nikon’s COOLSHOT pages describe aiming at a flagstick. They also say the unit can prefer the closer target when two things overlap, so a flag in front of trees is the number it is built to show. If you cannot see the flag, or you only want a number from the trees, there is nothing to aim at.

A GPS watch uses course data and where you are standing. It does not put a beam on the flag. The Approach S70 owner’s manual in English (opened November 3, 2026) says the watch shows distances to the front, middle, and back of the green, and not to the actual pin, because pin locations change. If the course data is wrong, the number moves with it. I did not find a distance-accuracy figure for the S70 on the manual pages I opened. 【Not on the pages opened: Approach S70 distance accuracy】

On my rounds I usually want the distance to the pin when I can see the flag. That is why the unit in my bag is a laser. I have no note of using a GPS watch on a round. I am not ranking one method above the other.

## Five checks: stabilization, weight, power, water, and slope on or off

Five checks, before any list of names. Nothing here is ordered by a score or by how many units a shop sold.

### Image stabilization

Nikon’s English PROIII page describes STABILIZED as reducing the shake you see in the finder and, at the same time, lining up the laser. The LITE page says finder shake from hand movement is reduced to 1/5 or less, under Nikon’s measurement standards. I am not copying that 1/5 figure onto the PROIII. The PROIII page I opened did not print it.

The PROIII page says the result is displayed in 0.1 second, and calls that the fastest in the COOLSHOT series. Those are Nikon’s words, not a ranking on this site. The same page says HYPER READ keeps that response regardless of distance. The LITE page says the result is displayed in approximately 0.3 seconds. Those seconds are Nikon’s, not a time I measured.

The Approach S70 is a watch. The English specification page I opened does not list optical image stabilization. 【Not on the pages opened: optical image stabilization on the Approach S70】

### Power

The PROIII and the LITE each take one CR2 lithium battery. Both specification tables list auto power shut-off after about 8 seconds unoperated. Neither page I opened calls the unit rechargeable.

The Approach S70 47 mm has a built-in rechargeable lithium-ion battery and charges from a cable into a USB port, according to the English manual. The battery page for that manual gives the 47 mm, under Garmin’s typical-use conditions, as up to 16 days in smartwatch mode and up to 20 hours in all-satellite-systems mode. The same page says real life depends on heart rate, notifications, GPS, and other sensors. The 42 mm column on that page is shorter (up to 10 days and up to 15 hours). I am not mixing the two sizes. I do not know how long a charge lasts on my own round, because I have not worn this watch.

### Water

The words are not one test.

- PROIII, imaging.nikon.com specification table: waterproof up to 1 m / 3.3 ft for 10 minutes, and fogproof. The battery chamber is rainproof, JIS/IEC protection class 4 (IPX4) equivalent, under Nikon’s testing. The Nikon USA overview I opened agrees on waterproof up to 1 m / 3.3 ft for 10 minutes.
- LITE, imaging.nikon.com: rainproof, IPX4 equivalent, under Nikon’s testing. The Nikon USA LITE overview, opened the same day, prints both a “waterproof up to 1 m / 3.3 ft for 10 minutes, fogproof” line and a separate “rainproof, IPX4” line. I am not folding those into one rating.
- Approach S70, English specification page: 5 ATM. The footnote says the device withstands pressure equivalent to a depth of 50 m, and points to Garmin’s water-rating page for more. I am not calling 5 ATM and IPX4 the same kind of rain.

### Slope on or off

A distance that has slope added in is treated differently in a casual round and in a competition. The rule is the next section. This section is only the switch the maker provides.

The PROIII English page lists four displays: golf mode (slope-adjusted distance and actual distance), actual distance mode, actual distance and height, and horizontal distance and height. It says an Actual Distance Indicator shows that the incline/decline function is not being used. The page I opened did not say what color that indicator is. 【Not on the English PROIII page opened: color of the Actual Distance Indicator】

The LITE English page lists two displays: actual distance mode, and golf mode (slope-adjusted distance and actual distance). It says actual distance mode can be used for official golf competitions. That is Nikon’s sentence, not a statement that every committee allows the device. In actual distance mode, the indicator blinks green while the power is on, so someone else can see that ID technology is off. Golf mode shows a guide distance of how far to hit the ball. The LITE page writes that guide as horizontal distance ± height.

The PROIII page describes ID technology as a guide to how far you should hit the ball on an uphill or downhill hole. I am not pasting the LITE formula onto the PROIII. The PROIII specification table does list a slope-adjusted distance as one of the displays.

The Approach S70 manual describes PlaysLike as an adjusted distance to the green that accounts for elevation, wind speed, wind direction, and air density. A PlaysLike setting can turn wind on, and another can turn air density on. The same golf-settings list includes Tournament Mode, which disables PinPointer and PlaysLike. The manual says those features are not allowed during sanctioned tournaments or handicap-calculation rounds. The Tournament Mode sentence I opened does not name Virtual Caddie. Virtual Caddie is a separate line, and it requests a club recommendation. 【Not in the Tournament Mode sentence opened: whether Virtual Caddie is switched off with it】

The Japanese manual, cited in the Japanese draft, has a longer Tournament Mode paragraph. I am not copying that longer paragraph into this page. A switch on the device is not the same thing as permission at a particular competition.

## Competition rules, from the USGA and the R&A

The Rules of Golf are written by the USGA and the R&A together. On November 3, 2026 I opened the R&A’s Rule 4 page and the 2023 Rules of Golf PDF. I also opened the USGA’s note on the change that made distance-measuring devices allowed unless a committee says otherwise. I am paraphrasing, not quoting the book at length. The rule text is the source.

Rule 4.3 is about how you use equipment during a round. It does not, by itself, make a list of banned gadgets you may not carry.

Rule 4.3a(1), distance and direction, splits like this:

- Allowed: getting information on distance or direction. A distance-measuring device and a compass are the examples on the page.
- Not allowed: measuring elevation changes. Interpreting distance or direction, and the example given is using a device to get a recommended line of play or a club selection based on where the ball is. Using an alignment device to line the ball up.

The penalty for a breach of Rule 4.3, on the same page: the first breach is the general penalty. If it happens between holes, it applies to the next hole. The second breach is disqualification, even when the second breach is a different kind of act, and only when something has intervened after the first breach (the page points to Rule 1.3c(4)). The 2023 Rules PDF defines the general penalty as loss of hole in match play and two penalty strokes in stroke play.

Model Local Rule G-5 lets the committee prohibit distance-measuring devices. The Rule 4 page points at Committee Procedures for G-5. A course or a competition can ban the device itself. Turning slope off does not override that. This draft does not say that any particular course allows one.

A slope-adjusted number, and a device that recommends a club, sit on the “not allowed” side of 4.3a(1). Actual distance mode on the Nikon units, and Tournament Mode on the Approach S70, are the switches the makers describe. Having the switch is not permission. The green blink on the LITE, or Tournament Mode on the watch, is something to check yourself before you tee off. I do not have a note of which display I use. 【Yonpii’s note: whether the straight-line display was checked in a competition = 〇〇】

Image stabilization does not appear in the Rule 4.3 text I opened. I am not mixing “easier to hold steady” with the rules.

PlaysLike, in the Garmin manual, includes elevation. Measuring elevation changes is one of the “not allowed” examples. It also can include wind. Rule 4.3a(2) allows weather information, including wind speed, from a forecast, and does not allow measuring wind speed at the course. The manual paragraph I opened did not say whether PlaysLike wind is a forecast or a measurement on the spot. 【Not confirmed: where PlaysLike gets its wind】

## Questions

### Can I leave slope on in a competition?

Rule 4.3a(1) puts measuring elevation changes, and using a device for a club recommendation, on the “not allowed” side. Nikon’s actual distance mode and Garmin’s Tournament Mode are the switches described above. If the committee has adopted Model Local Rule G-5, the device stays in the bag even with slope off. Read that competition’s local rule first.

### Does image stabilization break the rule?

The Rule 4.3 text I opened does not mention image stabilization. The uses that page marks as not allowed are the ones that measure elevation, or that turn a distance into a club or a line.

### Is the published weight the weight in a pocket?

The COOLSHOT figures, about 205 g and about 170 g, exclude the battery. The Approach S70 47 mm weight was not on the English specification page. My own number, with a battery or a strap, is still the blank line above.

### Why are most prices blank?

On November 3, 2026 the Nikon USA category page showed $529.95 for the COOLSHOT PROIII STABILIZED, and no price next to the LITE. The Garmin US product page did not return a current price. A 2023 newsroom line, “starting at $649.99,” is the series at launch, not a 47 mm price on that date. I am not writing a shop’s price into this table.
